Part 1

What's Already Built

Three phases are complete and working: the backend foundation, the live voice pipeline, and document knowledge search. This part records how each works, the code that matters, and every problem solved along the way.

The core loop

You speak into your browser. Your voice travels over a direct audio connection to the backend. A detector notices you are speaking and when you stop. Your speech becomes text. That text is used to search your uploaded documents, and anything relevant is added to the AI's instructions. The AI writes a reply. That reply becomes speech and travels back to your speaker. All of it happens in under two seconds.

Pipecat arranges processing steps in a line. Each step receives typed packets of data called frames, does something, and passes them along. Audio comes in one end, speech goes out the other.

How the audio connection actually works

  • Offer and answer — the browser describes what audio it can send, the backend replies with what it accepts.
  • Gathering addresses — both sides collect every network address they might be reachable on.
  • Connectivity checks — both sides send small packets to each other's addresses until one route works. This is exactly where the Windows Firewall was blocking things.

Phase 3 — Document knowledge

A large language model knows nothing about your documents. Retrieval works around this: text is converted into lists of numbers that capture meaning, stored in a searchable index, and at question time the closest matches are found and pasted into the AI's instructions before it answers.

backend/app/services/rag.py
def chunk_text(pages, chunk_size=400, overlap=80):
    chunks = []
    for page_num, text in pages:
        words = text.split()
        start = 0
        while start < len(words):
            chunk = " ".join(words[start:start + chunk_size])
            if len(chunk.strip()) > 50:
                chunks.append({"text": f"[Page {page_num}] {chunk}",
                               "page": page_num})
            start += chunk_size - overlap
    return chunks

async def query_context(bot_id, query, top_k=5):
    embeddings = await _embed([query])
    page_num = _extract_page_num(query)
    filter_dict = {"page": {"$eq": page_num}} if page_num else None
    results = index.query(vector=embeddings[0], top_k=top_k,
                          namespace=bot_id, include_metadata=True,
                          filter=filter_dict)
    return "\n\n".join(m.metadata["text"] for m in results.matches
                          if m.score > 0.2)

Every error hit, and how it was fixed

PhaseProblemRoot causeFix
1Frontend showed a blank white pageTypeScript interfaces do not exist at runtime, so importing one as a value crashed the moduleChanged to import type across all pages
2ModuleNotFoundError on the transportPipecat 1.7.0 moved the WebRTC transport to a new locationImport from pipecat.transports.smallwebrtc.transport
2ModuleNotFoundError on the LLM contextModule renamed in the same versionImport from llm_context and llm_response_universal
2Bot reacted to background noiseDetection thresholds were too permissiveRaised confidence to 0.6 and minimum volume to 0.25
2Connection timed out after 57 secondsWindows Firewall was blocking the UDP ports the audio layer needsAllowed python.exe through the firewall; added three STUN servers
2Hindi spoken with a neutral accentThe voice provider has no Punjabi voice; the language setting affects text handling, not accentAccepted as a known limitation; noted for a future provider change
3PDF upload returned a server errorThe Pinecone index did not exist yetCreated it with 1536 dimensions, cosine metric
3Delete button did nothing/documents was missing from the dev-server proxy, so requests never reached the backendAdded the route to vite.config.ts
3Search returned nothing at allThreshold was 0.45, but technical document chunks only score 0.2–0.3 against conversational speechLowered the threshold to 0.2
3Page numbers lost during parsingAll pages were merged into one block of text before chunkingParse page by page and prefix each chunk with its page number
3"Page fifty" was not recognisedThe pattern only matched digits, not spoken wordsAdded a word-to-number lookup and a second matching pattern
3Search fired on "Can", "Can you", "Can you tell"Deepgram streams partial results as you speak; search ran on every fragmentOnly run on the final transcription, never on partial ones
3Bot claimed it could not access filesThe instructions were too minimal, so the model's default behaviour overrode the supplied document textAdded an explicit instruction telling it the supplied text is the document content
2End Call needed several clicksThe stop function ran repeatedly with no guardAdded a check before closing the connection

What each error taught

Similarity scores are relativeA score of 0.25 is not "bad" — it depends entirely on the domain. Dense technical text against casual speech will always score lower than an FAQ against typed questions. Thresholds must be calibrated per use case, never copied.
Voice audio does not use normal portsReal-time audio runs over UDP, not the HTTP ports firewalls usually permit. Standard web rules do not cover it, which is why a relay server matters for real users.
Retrieval is half the problemEven with the right text supplied, the model ignored it until explicitly told it was permitted to use it. Prompt wording matters as much as search quality.
Partial results are a trapStreaming speech recognition emits fragments constantly. Anything expensive downstream must wait for the final result or it runs dozens of times per sentence.
Part 2

Giving It Hands

Right now the bot can only talk. Function calling is what lets it actually do things — and it is the single feature that turns a demo into a product.

The problem it solves

Today the bot can only discuss two things: what is in its personality instructions, and what is in your uploaded PDFs. It cannot check a live database, cannot book anything, cannot look up your specific order. It is a very good talker with no hands.

How it works

You describe your available functions to the AI in plain English:

What the AI is told it can do
Available tools:
  get_order_status(order_id)   → Look up delivery status of an order
  book_appointment(date, time) → Reserve a slot in the calendar
  transfer_to_human()          → Hand the call to a live agent

Now the AI has a choice. When it needs real data, instead of replying with words, it replies with a request to run one of your functions.

A real conversation, step by step

What actually happens
CALLER speaks:  "Hey, where's my order 4471?"
                        ↓
AI thinks:      "I don't know this. But I have a tool for it."
                  get_order_status(order_id="4471")
                        ↓
YOUR PYTHON:    Runs the function. Queries your real database
                  or the shipping company's API.
                  Returns: { status: "shipped",
                             eta: "tomorrow 6pm",
                             carrier: "BlueDart" }
                        ↓
AI receives it, writes a normal answer:
                  "Your order shipped and should arrive tomorrow
                   evening around 6, with BlueDart."
                        ↓
SPOKEN ALOUD — the caller never knows a database was queried.

Is it like calling customer care?

Yes — that is exactly the end goal. But it is two separate pieces, and separating them is useful:

Function calling = the brainThe ability to look things up and take actions. Without this, even on a real phone line the bot could only chat — it could not tell you where your package is.
A phone number = the lineWhat lets someone dial a normal number and reach the bot, instead of opening a browser tab.

"Hi, thanks for calling." — "I want to return a headphone I bought last week." — (the bot silently looks up orders by your phone number) — "I see a delivery on the 19th. Part 3

Zero Budget, Enterprise Ready

Your requirement: no money to spend now, but the finished product must be good enough that a company buys it and can run the paid version with minimal changes from your end. This is how that works.

The swap-point architecture

Every external service the platform talks to — speech recognition, the AI model, the voice, the audio transport — sits behind a small factory function that reads one environment variable. You build and test against the free column. A paying client flips the variable to the paid column. The pipeline code never changes, because these services already share a common interface.

ComponentFree — build with thisPaid — client flips to this
Speech to textWhisper (faster-whisper, local)→Deepgram / AssemblyAI
Language modelGroq (Llama 3.3, free tier)→OpenAI GPT-4o / Claude
Text to speechPiper / Kyutai Pocket TTS→Cartesia / ElevenLabs
Audio transportLiveKit self-hosted (free VM)→Daily / LiveKit Cloud
Vector databaseQdrant self-hosted→Pinecone
HostingOracle Cloud free VM→AWS / GCP / Fly.io
.env — this is the entire "upgrade"
# Client flips four lines. No code changes anywhere.
STT_PROVIDER=deepgram        # was: whisper_local
LLM_PROVIDER=openai          # was: groq
TTS_PROVIDER=cartesia        # was: piper
TRANSPORT_PROVIDER=daily     # was: livekit_selfhost
Why this is the pitch: you are not selling a demo a company has to rebuild. You are selling infrastructure that is already production-shaped. Their engineering cost to go live is close to zero — which is exactly what makes it purchasable from a solo builder with no funding behind him.
One honest correction: self-hosting stops being the cheap option somewhere around a few hundred concurrent calls. Self-hosted Whisper needs GPUs to keep up, and a GPU fleet costs more than per-minute cloud speech recognition until you are very large. So the free stack is genuinely for building and for small clients — at scale the paid providers are both faster and cheaper. That is fine: by then the client is paying, and the swap point is what makes that transition painless.

What's Still Missing

Honest assessment of every gap between "impressive demo" and "purchasable product". Blocker means a serious buyer walks away. High means you lose deals or cannot support customers. Medium means it limits who can buy.

Money and customers

GapSeverityWhat it is, and what happens without it
Billing and meteringBlockerMeasuring what each customer consumes and charging for it. Without it: you have no way to take money at all. You can demo but you cannot sell.
Company accounts and rolesBlockerAn organisation account where ten employees share bots with different permission levels. Without it: a company literally cannot adopt it — everyone would share one login, which no IT department allows.
Spending capsHighA hard ceiling on what any customer can cost you, plus anomaly alerts. Without it: one bug or abusive user runs up thousands overnight and you learn from the invoice.
Admin panelHighA screen to see customers, inspect a call, suspend an account. Without it: every support request means connecting to a server and reading logs by hand.

Legal and trust

GapSeverityWhat it is, and what happens without it
Recording consentBlockerTelling callers they are recorded, per-customer toggles, retention policy. Without it: recording without disclosure is illegal in many places. Legal exposure, not a missing feature.
Removing sensitive dataBlockerMasking card and ID numbers before transcripts are saved. Without it: your database fills with card numbers in plain text and a small breach becomes a catastrophe.
Export and deletionBlockerIndia's DPDP Act and Europe's GDPR both require genuine erasure — database, search index, logs, backups. Without it: you cannot legally serve business customers.
Audit logHighAn unchangeable record of who did what and when. Without it: you fail security reviews and cannot prove what happened.
Company login (SSO)HighSigning in with existing Google or Microsoft accounts. Without it: a straight disqualification on many enterprise checklists.
Security hygieneHighDependency and secret scanning, encryption at rest. Without it: an instant red flag in any technical review. Cheap to add.

Safety and quality

GapSeverityWhat it is, and what happens without it
GuardrailsBlockerStopping the bot being talked into promising refunds, insulting people, or revealing its instructions. Without it: one screenshot of your bot saying something damaging ends that relationship.
Conversation testingHighAutomated tests where an AI plays the customer. Different from document-search testing. Without it: you change a prompt, quality drops, and you find out from a complaint weeks later.
Call replayHighA per-call timeline of speech, retrieved passages, tool calls and timings. Without it: "why did the bot say that?" is unanswerable — and it is the most common question you will get.
Prompt versioningMediumSaving every version so you can compare and roll back. Without it: one careless edit silently degrades every conversation with no undo.
Latency percentilesMediumTracking p50/p95/p99, not averages. Without it: the average looks healthy while 5% of callers have a terrible time and hang up.
Escalation triggersMediumDetecting frustration or repeated failure and handing over. Without it: a stuck bot traps the caller in a loop, worse than no bot at all.

Every Question A Buyer Will Ask

When a company evaluates buying software, several different teams review it and each has its own checklist. These are their questions, what they are really checking, and what you need in place. Tick a question when you can answer it confidently — that feeds the READY tracker at the top.

Use this while building, not after. Most of these have answers that must be designed in — data deletion, audit logs, tenant isolation. Retrofitting them means rewriting things you already built. Building with the questions in mind costs almost nothing extra.

The ten that kill deals most often

#QuestionWhy it is fatal
1Where is our data stored, and can it stay in India / the EU?Data residency is often a hard legal requirement, not a preference.
2Can we permanently delete all our data, and how quickly?Required by law. "We'll mark it deleted" is not an acceptable answer.
3Is our data used to train AI models?The expected answer is a clear no, in writing, including for your suppliers.
4How is our data kept separate from other customers'?They want enforced isolation, not "we filter by customer ID".
5Can the bot be tricked into saying something damaging?They will test this personally during the trial. Assume it.
6What is your uptime guarantee, and what if you miss it?Needs a number, a public status page, and a stated remedy.
7Do you support login with our company accounts?Frequently mandatory above about 200 employees.
8What happens if your AI provider goes down mid-call?They are testing whether you have thought about failure at all.
9Can we cap our monthly spend?Nobody signs an agreement with unlimited financial exposure.
10You are one person — what happens to us if you stop?The hardest and most legitimate question. Have a real answer ready.
The one question you must have a real answer to: "You are one person — what happens to us if you stop?" Do not brush it off. Strong answers: their data is exportable at any time in a standard format; the deployment is documented well enough that another engineer could run it; source code escrow is available for larger contracts; and you are transparent about what support they can and cannot expect. Buyers respect a solo vendor who has clearly thought about this far more than one who acts as though it is not a fair question.

Doing Several Things At Once

Multi-tasking means two different things and both need building. One is inside a single conversation. The other is across thousands of users at once, which is Part 11.

CapabilityWhat the caller experiencesWhy it matters
Parallel tool calls"Book the cab and text me the receipt" — both fire at the same instantTwo sequential API calls take twice as long, and in a voice conversation that delay is very noticeable.
Background tasks"I'm booking that now — while it processes, what address should I use?"A slow API no longer means dead air. Ten seconds of silence on a call makes people hang up.
Multi-step workflowsBook flight → book hotel → send itinerary, all from one requestThis is what makes it an assistant rather than a single-function bot.
Saga rollbackHotel fails after the flight is booked — the flight is cancelled automaticallyWithout it, a half-finished workflow leaves real charges and real mess on a real customer's card.
Human approval"That refund is ₹40,000 — I'm sending it to a manager for approval."High-value actions need a gate. Enterprises will insist on this before letting it handle anything.

Saga rollback is the one people skip. It is easy to build a bot that calls three APIs in a row. It is much harder to build one that correctly undoes the first two when the third fails — and with real third-party APIs, failure happens constantly. Building compensation logic into the tool framework from the start is far cheaper than retrofitting it after a customer gets double-charged.
Some things genuinely cannot be undone — a sent message, a charged card. For those, do not pretend. Record it, tell the caller plainly what did and did not complete, and where money is involved escalate to a human. Silently leaving a mess is much worse than admitting it.
Part 11

10 Lakh Users Without Falling Over

You asked twice about scale, and then asked directly whether it can serve 10 lakh people simultaneously. Here is the honest answer, the maths behind it, and the architecture that gets you there.

The two numbers people mix up

NumberMeaningYour realistic figure
Registered usersPeople who have an account with you10,00,000 ✓ achievable
Simultaneous callsPeople speaking to the bot right now, this second~5,000 – 20,000
Can it serve 10 lakh people simultaneously, all on calls at once? No — and no system on earth currently does. That would mean every single registered user picking up the phone in the same second. It does not happen for any product. Not WhatsApp, not your bank, not Amazon.

What 10 lakh simultaneous calls would actually cost

RequirementFigure

Per-minute cost~$0.04 per call-minute (speech, AI, voice, transport, compute combined)
Total per minute$40,000 per minute ≈ ₹35 lakh per minute
Per hour≈ ₹21 crore

Bandwidth~64 Gbps sustained, both directions

For perspective: a large enterprise call centre runs 5,000–10,000 agents. Major cloud contact-centre platforms serve huge enterprises, but individual customers rarely exceed a few thousand concurrent. 10 lakh simultaneous voice calls would make you one of the largest real-time voice systems on the planet — you would be asking for a noticeable percentage of an entire country's call capacity.

What 10 lakh registered users actually needs

ScenarioConcurrent callsServers needed
Normal daily use (0.5% active)~5,000~95
Launch day / campaign (2%)~20,000~375
Extreme viral spike (5%)~50,000~940

Design for 20,000. Handle 50,000 with a queue. That is a completely normal engineering problem.

The insight that actually matters: peak concurrency determines your infrastructure, total minutes determine your bill. For 10 lakh users making 2 calls a month at 3 minutes each: 60,00,000 minutes × $0.04 ≈ $240,000/month variable cost. Charge even $1/user/month and you have $1M revenue. The margin works — and it never requires 10 lakh simultaneous anything.

Cell-based architecture — the key design

A single autoscaling pool is fine at 1 lakh users and dangerous at 10 lakh: one bad deploy or one poisoned queue takes down every customer at once. The fix is to stop running one big system and run twenty identical small ones, each serving about 50,000 users.

The one case where huge concurrency is real: outbound campaigns. If a client says "call all 5 lakh of our customers about this offer", you control when those fire — so they genuinely could all start at once. The answer is not infinite parallel capacity. It is pacing: spread the campaign over hours, add a configurable calls-per-minute limit, and let the client choose. "5 lakh calls over 8 hours" is about 1,000 concurrent — entirely manageable. That is how every real dialler system works.
What to tell a customer who asks: "The platform is designed for 20,000 simultaneous conversations and scales linearly beyond that by adding capacity. For 10 lakh registered users that gives comfortable headroom even on a launch-day spike. For outbound campaigns we pace delivery to your chosen rate rather than dialling everyone at once." Honest, technically correct, and far more credible than claiming a number nobody could verify or afford.
Does the architecture cap out at 20,000? No. Cells scale linearly — 20 cells for 10 lakh users, 200 cells for 1 crore. Same code, same design, just more cells. The architecture is not your limit. The bill is. And if you genuinely had 10 lakh people on calls at once, you would be generating ₹35 lakh per minute in revenue — you would be a large company with a large team, and the money would fund the infrastructure.

Can It Search The Internet Live?

You asked whether it can connect to the internet for real-time information. Yes, comfortably.

It is completely feasible and it is just another tool call. Once the function-calling framework from Phase 1 exists, adding web search is roughly two hours of work. The bot decides on its own when it needs current information, searches, reads the results, and answers — all inside the conversation.

How it works

1Caller asks something you cannot know in advance — "what's today's gold rate?", "is my flight delayed?"
2The AI recognises it has no answer and calls your search_web(query) tool instead of guessing.
3Your Python code queries a search API and gets back a handful of relevant snippets.
4Those snippets go back to the AI, which reads them and composes a spoken answer.
5The caller hears a natural reply that happens to contain live information.

Which search service

OptionWhy pick itCost shape
TavilyPurpose-built for AI agents — returns clean summarised snippets rather than raw pages, so the AI gets usable text immediatelyFree tier, then per search
Brave Search APIIndependent index, generous free tier, privacy-friendly. Good default while buildingFree tier available
ExaSemantic search — good when the question is conceptual rather than keyword-shapedFree tier, then per search
Direct APIsFor specific live data — weather, flights, stock prices — a dedicated API beats general search every timeVaries

The four things that will bite you

ProblemWhat goes wrongFix
Silence while searchingA search takes one to three seconds. On a phone call that is a very long, very awkward gap.Speak a filler immediately — "let me check that for you" — while the search runs in the background.
Made-up answersThe AI blends search results with its own assumptions and states something wrong with total confidence.Instruct it explicitly to answer only from the snippets, and to say it could not find out otherwise.
Untrustworthy sourcesIt picks up a random blog and repeats it as fact on behalf of a brand.Restrict searches to an allowed list of domains per bot. Most business cases need only a few.
Runaway costThe bot searches on every turn, including "hello" and "thank you".Make it a tool the AI chooses, never automatic. Cache repeated queries for a few minutes.
The bigger opportunity is not general web search. For most business customers the valuable live data is their own — order status, stock levels, account balances, appointment slots. That is the same tool mechanism pointed at their systems instead of a search engine, and it is worth far more to them. Build web search because it is easy and demos well; build live connections to their data because that is what they will pay for.

Ideas That Solve Real Problems

Anyone can build a voice bot that answers questions from a PDF. These are angles that solve genuine problems, are underserved by the big players, and would make this product distinctive rather than merely competent.

The strategic point: OpenAI, Google and the well-funded US startups are all optimising for American English business calls. The gap they are not filling is everything else — other languages, mixed languages, bad networks, basic phones, and the hundreds of millions of people those exclude. That gap is on your doorstep, and it is where a small builder can genuinely beat a large one.

The strongest three

IdeaThe real-world problem it solvesWhy you can win it
Live translation between Indian languages
Flagship		 A customer speaks Tamil. The support agent speaks only Hindi. The bot sits in the middle and translates both directions in real time, live on the call. India has 22 official languages and hundreds of millions of people who cannot get service in theirs. Banks, telecoms, hospitals, insurers and government offices face this daily and currently solve it by hiring separate teams per language, or by simply not serving those callers well. Streaming translation across 70+ languages now exists as an API — the hard part is done. What is missing is someone applying it properly to Indian languages, with the right latency and accent handling.
Native Hinglish and code-switching
Underserved		 Real Indian speech mixes languages inside a single sentence: "mujhe apna balance check karna hai please". Nearly every voice AI handles this badly, because they are built to detect one language and stick to it. Handling it properly means detecting mid-sentence switching, keeping meaning across the switch, and replying in the same mixed register the caller used — not correcting them into formal Hindi or formal English. This is how a billion people actually talk, and getting it right is an immediately demonstrable advantage in any demo to an Indian company.
Works on a basic phone
Impact + market		 No smartphone, no app, no reading required. Just a plain phone call over a weak network. For a large number of people this is the only interface they have — and voice is the only one that does not require literacy. Means designing for patchy 2G: handling three-second network drops without ending the call, degrading audio quality gracefully instead of failing, and keeping conversation state so it can resume. Genuine social value and a real market — rural banking, crop and weather information, government schemes, and health services all need exactly this and have budget for it.

The Brand Name Problem

You asked me to check whether the name is registered in the US. I did. You were right to check.

"Auris" is genuinely crowded — and one conflict is serious. There is an active company called Auris AI at aurisvoiceai.com selling a business voice assistant that schedules appointments and answers questions. That is your exact product in your exact category. Separately, Auris Health is a Johnson & Johnson surgical robotics subsidiary acquired for $3.4 billion, and there is another Auris in London doing speech recognition for children's voices — also voice AI. The USPTO word mark "AURIS" registered to Acrisure shows as dead/abandoned, but that does not help you: in the US, trademark rights come from actual commercial use, not only from the register. A competitor actively trading under the name in your category is the real obstacle.

What this actually means

  • You are not in trouble today. Nothing bad has happened. You have a project, not a market presence.
  • Renaming now is cheap. Renaming after you have customers, contracts, a domain and marketing is expensive and disruptive.
  • The risk is real but not dramatic. The realistic outcome of a clash is a demand that you stop using the name — after you have built brand value into it.
  • I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. Run a proper search before you commit. Steps below.

All of these lean on sound, speech or hearing without the crowded Latin root. The Sanskrit-derived ones have a real advantage: distinctive, meaningful, far less likely to collide in US tech, and they give you an origin story that is genuinely yours rather than borrowed.

How to check a name properly

StepWhat to do

2. Indian trademark registerSearch on IP India's public search. If you operate from India, this is where you would actually file.
3. Plain web searchThe most important step, and the one people skip. Search "name AI", "name voice", "name software". An active competitor matters more than the register.
